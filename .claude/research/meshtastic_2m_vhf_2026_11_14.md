# Meshtastic reaches 2 m; MeshForge has catching up to do

Meshtastic has built real 2 m support, and it is more than a proposal. Between April and October 2026 the core team merged three ITU-split 2 m regions (`ITU1_2M` 144–146 MHz, `ITU2_2M` and `ITU3_2M` 144–148 MHz). They added two 15.6 kHz "TINY" presets that sit in 20 kHz slots, a licensed-mode gate that strips encryption and refuses to relay to or from unlicensed nodes, XEdDSA-signed plaintext for hams, and two LilyGO SX1278 boards, one of them the ~5 W T-Beam BPF (**VERIFIED** from commits and code read in the research session). The independent community record confirms the merges but adds almost nothing on top. No published 2 m LoRa range or SNR measurement exists, no regulator or national society has commented, and the only shipping silicon that covers 144 MHz within its datasheet is the legacy SX127x family. All of this ships only in **alpha** firmware: every 2.8 tag is a pre-release, three of the five have been revoked, and the stable channel is still 2.7.26 (**VERIFIED** from the releases page). For a US General like WH6GXZ, the 15.6 kHz presets fit the §97.307(f)(5) 20 kHz cap for unspecified digital codes (**BELIEVED**: rule text seen in snippets, not opened). MeshForge is not ready. Its one firmware-accurate region table has no 2 m entry, and its slot formula would put the US default channel about 220 kHz off frequency. `is_licensed` is parsed but never shown. Its bridge has no notion of an amateur leg, so it could inject AES-encrypted traffic onto 2 m. Two 2.8 findings reach past 2 m. The 2.8.2 tag builds DMShell, a remote shell over the mesh, into every Linux meshtasticd, switches it on, and authorises the local API on any unmanaged node. Develop made it opt-in on 2026-11-10, and the fleet's 2.7.26 hold keeps it off the boxes (**VERIFIED** in source). 2.8 also removes MQTT JSON from every build, and MeshForge's JSON telemetry decoder already reads a nested shape that 2.7.26 firmware never emits (**VERIFIED** by code read on both sides; the live effect is **BELIEVED**). Sensors are the biggest MeshForge gap. Upstream 2.8 adds a soil-and-water variant, lightning, ADC and one-wire fields, and shell-command sensors on Linux. MeshForge partly decodes four of nine telemetry variants, keeps no per-node sensor history and shows no reading age, and it cannot configure the telemetry module from the TUI. The work below is ordered to respect the roadmap rule against new edges until the two-leg bridge is honest.

## The double tap: GitHub and the community agree on what merged, and the community has nothing to add on performance

The primary record is unambiguous. Protobufs PR #897 (Thomas Göttgens, 2026-04-21) introduced ITU amateur regions. PRs #922/#923 (Austin, GitHub `vidplace7`, merged 2026-05-26) split them by ITU region ([protobufs #922](https://github.com/meshtastic/protobufs/pull/922), [#923](https://github.com/meshtastic/protobufs/pull/923)), and #934 added `TINY_FAST = 14` / `TINY_SLOW = 15` ([protobufs #934](https://github.com/meshtastic/protobufs/pull/934)). On the firmware side, **PR #10623 (merged 2026-06-03)** added the 2 m regions with default slots ITU1 26 = 144.510 MHz, ITU2 51 = **145.010 MHz** and ITU3 33 = 144.650 MHz ([firmware #10623](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10623)). Then came the T-Beam Supreme 144 MHz variant on 2026-06-04 ([#10624](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10624)), BaseUI Ham Mode on 06-09, which keeps TX disabled while the callsign is N0CALL ([#10663](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10663)), and licensed-mode plaintext signing on 07-26 ([#10969](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10969)). The dedicated **T-Beam BPF** followed on 07-27 ([#10558](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10558)), with PSK stripping on licensed defaults on 07-30 ([#11286](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11286)) and refusal of key verification in ham mode on 10-19 ([#12201](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12201)). An older rule from 2026-03-22, **#9958**, stops licensed nodes rebroadcasting packets to or from unlicensed users ([#9958](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/9958)). Clients followed: Android added the 2.8 amateur regions and a licensed-mode warning ([Android #6548](https://github.com/meshtastic/Meshtastic-Android/pull/6548), [#6632](https://github.com/meshtastic/Meshtastic-Android/pull/6632)), and web filters presets by region ([web 5ffd1b0](https://github.com/meshtastic/web/commit/5ffd1b0)). No ham-specific Apple commit was found. `git tag --contains` places every 2 m commit first in `v2.8.0.7239fe8` and none in the 2.7 branch (**VERIFIED**).

The community view confirms the same story from outside. The 2.8 punchlist issue #10462 lists "~144 MHz 2 m, 1.25 m, 70 cm" regions and the T-Beam Supreme 144 ([Issue #10462](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/issues/10462)). The Hamvention 2026 flasher labels the T-Beam BPF "Amateur radio band — an amateur radio license is required to transmit" ([hamvention.meshtastic.org, snippet](https://hamvention.meshtastic.org/)), and NodakMesh describes Ham Mode's N0CALL lockout ([NodakMesh, snippet](https://nodakmesh.org/blog/meshtastic-ham-mode-amateur-regions)). Both taps agree on **what** merged. Neither has field data on how it **performs**. PR #10623 attests only to regression testing on `tlora-v2-1-1_6` and `t-beam-bpf`. The only pre-2026 builder report, SQ6RDP's T3 144 MHz board, says only that it "works and transmits" ([LoRa_APRS_iGate #250](https://github.com/richonguzman/LoRa_APRS_iGate/discussions/250)). The one quantified VHF LoRa range figure (15–20 km LOS at 144.415 MHz, SF8/10.4 kHz) appears to be an estimate, not a measurement ([Hamradio.my, snippet](https://hamradio.my/2024/09/exploring-lora-aprs-vhf-vs-uhf-performance/)). PR #10623 contains no band-plan discussion at all: nothing on APRS, satellites or repeaters. Any 2 m range claim in late 2026 is therefore physics, not observation.

One framing in the primary notes needs correcting. "Merged and shipped" is accurate only for develop and the alpha channel. The firmware releases page lists v2.8.2.d5f1619 (04 Nov) as the live alpha. v2.8.2.4c89b69, v2.8.0.47db0e3 and v2.8.0.7239fe8 are **revoked**, and **v2.7.26.54e0d8d remains "Latest"** ([firmware releases](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/releases)). The project's own blog calls 2.8.1 "not ready for wide deployment" ([blog source](https://github.com/meshtastic/meshtastic/blob/master/blog/2026/October/lets-talk-about-2-8.mdx)). 2 m Meshtastic is an alpha feature.

The firmware `RadioInterface.cpp` table is the authoritative definition (**VERIFIED**, quoted verbatim in the research notes) ([RadioInterface.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meshtastic/firmware/develop/src/mesh/RadioInterface.cpp)):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ITU1_2M (27) | 144.0–146.0 | 100 % | 30 dBm | HAM_20KHZ | TINY_FAST | 26 → 144.510 |
| ITU2_2M (28) | 144.0–148.0 | 100 % | 30 dBm | HAM_20KHZ | TINY_FAST | 51 → 145.010 |
| ITU3_2M (33) | 144.0–148.0 | 100 % | 30 dBm | HAM_20KHZ | TINY_FAST | 33 → 144.650 |
| ITU2_125CM (37) | 220.0–225.0 | 100 % | 30 dBm | HAM_100KHZ | NARROW_SLOW | 37 |
| ITU2_70CM (35) | 420.0–450.0 | 100 % | 30 dBm | HAM_100KHZ | NARROW_SLOW | 137 |

`PROFILE_HAM_20KHZ = {PRESETS_TINY, 0, 0.0022f, false, true, 0, 2, 2}` sets 2.2 kHz of padding on each side of the 15.6 kHz signal. It disables audio, makes the region licensed-only, and doubles the position and telemetry throttle. TINY_FAST is **SF7/CR4/5 at 15.6 kHz**, TINY_SLOW is **SF8/CR4/6** ([MeshRadio.h](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/mesh/MeshRadio.h)). The docs list them at 0.68 and 0.33 kbps ([radio-settings.mdx](https://github.com/meshtastic/meshtastic/blob/master/docs/about/overview/radio-settings.mdx)), which matches SF·BW/2^SF·CR (my arithmetic). The proto comment states that a **±5 ppm-or-better TCXO is "absolutely required"** ([config.proto](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meshtastic/protobufs/master/meshtastic/config.proto)). A proposed `trident` registry renumbers RegionCode ([protobufs trident](https://github.com/meshtastic/protobufs/tree/trident)), so the enum values above hold only for the 2.8 wire format.

## Building a 2 m node means SX1278, a TCXO, a 7-pole LPF and a 49 cm antenna

**Silicon decides almost everything.** The SX1276/77/78 family covers 137 MHz and up, which includes 2 m within spec ([Semtech LoRa product guide](https://www.semtech.com/uploads/design-support/SEMTECH_LORA_PG.pdf)). Every newer part, including SX1261/62/68, LLCC68, LR1110/1121 and LR2021, starts at **150 MHz** ([SX1262 datasheet](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/6/b/5/1/4/SX1262_datasheet.pdf), [LR2021](https://www.semtech.com/products/wireless-rf/lora-plus/lr2021)). Both in-tree 144 MHz targets use the SX1278 through the RF95 driver. The T-Beam BPF `variant.h` defines `USE_RF95`, `HAS_HAM_2M_ONLY 1`, `RF95_RXEN 39` (LNA enable), `RF95_POWER_EN 16` and `RF95_MAX_POWER 10` ([variant.h](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/variants/esp32s3/t-beam-bpf/variant.h)). The firmware carries no per-chip frequency bounds; the only hardware check is sub-GHz versus 2.4 GHz capability. A 915 MHz SX1262 board set to `ITU2_2M` would therefore try to tune 145 MHz into a mismatched filter. Region choice is policy-gated, not hardware-gated (**BELIEVED** from reading).

**The "SX1262 floor versus hobbyist PLL" conflict resolves in favour of the datasheet for on-air use.** The SX1262_CHIRP project reports the PLL "happy from somewhere around 50 MHz up through 1300 MHz". It also reports that with a 900 MHz match the odd harmonics fall "only 10–15 dB down from the fundamental instead of 40+ dB" ([SX1262_CHIRP](https://github.com/ibelinp/SX1262_CHIRP)). A more careful bench project treats PLL lock, RSSI change and calibrated reception as separate evidence levels ([SX1262-SDR, snippet](https://github.com/hardcoreerik/SX1262-SDR)). RadioLib reportedly rejects SX126x frequencies below 150 MHz ([RadioLib SX1262 ref, snippet](https://jgromes.github.io/RadioLib/class_s_x1262.html)). PLL lock is a receive-side curiosity. It is not a transmitter you can legally key on 2 m without a spectrum analyzer. The proto comment that TINY presets suit "SX127x and SX126x" most plausibly targets SX126x at 220/430 MHz or a 150 MHz+ override (**BELIEVED**, inference). Ebyte's E22-170M parts are SPI SX1262 at 150.125–170.125 MHz, above the band ([Ebyte E22-170M33S](https://ebyteiot.com/products/ebyte-e22-170m33s-23dbm-16km-170-125mhz-spi-sx1262-module)). The E32-170 UART modules cannot run Meshtastic at all, because they hide the radio registers behind vendor firmware.

**The front end is where 2 m is harder than 915.** The third harmonic of 144–148 MHz lands at **432–444 MHz**, inside the 70 cm band and on the 435–438 MHz satellite uplinks (my calculation). For a mean power under 25 W, §97.307(e) requires spurs ≤ 25 µW (−16 dBm) and at least 40 dBc (**BELIEVED**, training knowledge, re-verify on eCFR). At the T-Beam BPF's 37 dBm that means **≥53 dBc**, and a 50 W brick moves into the 60 dBc rule. A 5-pole Butterworth at fc = 160 MHz gives 25.5 dB at the 2nd harmonic and 43.1 dB at the 3rd. A 7-pole gives 35.7 and 60.4 dB. So the plan is a 7-pole Chebyshev or an elliptic filter with notches at 288 and 432 MHz, plus a 2 m BPF ahead of the LNA against pager (152–159 MHz) and NOAA (162 MHz) blocking (own calculations). **TCXO stability is easy at VHF:** 1 ppm is 145 Hz, and ±BW/4 offset tolerance at 15.6 kHz allows about ±27 ppm total. The ±5 ppm requirement is comfortable for a ±0.5–2.5 ppm TCXO and marginal for a bare crystal across temperature. The SX127x takes the TCXO on XTA with RegTcxo set, unlike the SX126x DIO3 scheme (**BELIEVED**, training). To re-match an SX127x LF port from a 433 MHz module, scale L and C by about 3×. Starting from Semtech's 169 MHz reference and scaling by 1.17× is quicker. LoRa is constant-envelope, so a saturated class-C/E PA or an existing FM brick works. An FM brick needs hard T/R sequencing from RXEN, because RF-sense switching clips preambles.

**The power figures conflict, and the variant file settles which number is measured.** The table limit is 30 dBm. The BPF PR description says "+27 dBm radio", a squash commit says "This is a 5-6W radio!!", and the title read "~37 dBm (5 Watts)" until it was renamed on 2026-08-30 ([PR #10558](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10558)). `variant.h` records measured output on 5 V USB of 0, 34, 35, 36, 36, 37, 37, 38, 38, 38 dBm across PA steps and states 37 dBm maximum on battery. The reading that reconciles all of these is a **+27 dBm-rated PA stage label, with ~34–38 dBm at the antenna port as measured by the developer**. Independent verification is still **UNKNOWN**, since no third-party harmonic or power measurement exists. The 30 dBm cap does not bind a licensed operator. `if ((power == 0) || ((power > newRegion->powerLimit) && !devicestate.owner.is_licensed))` clamps only unlicensed nodes, and the ITU2 comment notes that "US FCC Part 97 allow[s] well above 30 dBm for licensed operators" (**BELIEVED** from reading code, not run). The 30 dBm figure is the unlicensed fallback and the ITU1 regulatory comment, not a ceiling for WH6GXZ.

### The link budget favours fixed rural nodes, not handhelds in town

At equal antenna gain, 145 MHz has **16.0 dB less free-space path loss than 915 MHz** and 9.5 dB less than 433 MHz. Man-made noise eats much of that. The ITU-R P.372 model gives residential Fa ≈ 12.6 dB at 145 MHz, so a 6 dB-NF receiver sees about 13.3 dB system noise figure. The same receiver at 915 MHz is effectively thermal-limited (P.372 coefficients **BELIEVED**, not re-fetched). Net of noise, the 2 m advantage over 915 MHz is about **+9 dB residential, +13 dB rural and +16 dB quiet rural**. Knife-edge diffraction adds about 8 dB in deep shadow (own calculations). Put a −6 dBi rubber duck on one end and the advantage over 433 MHz in town goes negative (assumed antenna gains, not sourced). A λ/4 is 49 cm, a λ/2 is 98 cm and a 5/8λ is 1.29 m.

There is one comparison the notes did not draw out. **TINY_FAST's symbol time (2^7/15.6 kHz = 8.2 ms) is essentially identical to LONG_FAST's (2^11/250 kHz = 8.2 ms).** At thermal noise TINY_FAST is about 2 dB more sensitive: −174 + 41.9 + 6 − 7.5 = −133.6 dBm, against −131.5 dBm for LONG_FAST. Because it carries 7 rather than 11 bits per symbol, a 60-byte packet takes roughly 1.26 s against 0.68 s for LONG_FAST, assuming a 16-symbol preamble and explicit header (my calculation). For a 5 W PA that means more than a second of key-down per packet. Add the doubled position and telemetry throttle and 2 m nodes will feel much slower than the US 915 MHz mesh. The real gain comes from **TX power headroom plus diffraction**, not raw FSPL.

An open-hardware BOM estimate lands at **~$15–30 for a bare 22 dBm node and ~$35–70 for a 5 W node with PA, LNA and filters**. The parts are an ESP32-S3 or nRF52840, an SX1276/78, a 32 MHz TCXO, a match and T/R switch, a 7-pole LPF, a PMU and a whip (training-era prices, **not sourced** this session). On the DC side, a 5 W PA at 50 % efficiency pulls about 2.7 A from a 3.7 V cell during TX.

## Regulation permits 15.6 kHz in the US; band plans are the open problem

**US Part 97.** Data on 144.1–148.0 MHz falls under §97.307(f)(5). Specified digital codes are capped at 19.6 kbaud, and **unspecified digital codes, which is where LoRa CSS falls, are capped at 20 kHz bandwidth** ([govinfo CFR 2025 §97.307](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title47-vol5/pdf/CFR-2025-title47-vol5-sec97-307.pdf)). FCC 23-93 (2023-11-13) removed baud limits only on HF. Removal on VHF/UHF was left to the FNPRM ([FCC 23-93](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-23-93A1.pdf)). ARRL asked again in April 2025 ([ARRL](https://www.arrl.org/news/arrl-files-comments-responding-to-fcc-request-for-input)), and no final order was found as of 2026-11-14. TINY_FAST's **15.6 kHz fits under 20 kHz** with 2.2 kHz of padding each side. 20.8 kHz LoRa does not, and 62.5 kHz and 125 kHz are not permitted on US 2 m. All of this is **BELIEVED**: the rule text was read from snippets because eCFR was unreachable. Because the FNPRM could change the cap, MeshForge should store it as data, not as a hardcode.

**Encryption, ID, automatic control and third parties.** Meshtastic's licensed mode implements §97.113(a)(4)'s ban on messages "encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning". `Channels::ensureLicensedOperation()` zeroes every channel PSK and disables the admin channel, `CryptoEngine::ensurePkiKeys` returns false, and packets are signed but not encrypted ([Channels.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/mesh/Channels.cpp), [CryptoEngine.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/mesh/CryptoEngine.cpp)) (**VERIFIED** from code). Signing is widely read as permitted, but no FCC ruling on Ed25519 signatures was found. **Station ID is a real ambiguity.** §97.119(b)(3) allows ID in a digital code listed in §97.309(a), LoRa is not on that list, and whether a NodeInfo callsign satisfies the rule is unresolved (**BELIEVED**, from memory). The repo's earlier Part 97 research quotes §97.221(b) as permitting automatic control of digital stations on "6 m or shorter" bands, which includes 2 m (`.claude/research/dudeclaw_role_usecases_2026_06_21.md:153-178`). On third-party traffic, my training knowledge is that §97.115(c) bars third-party traffic under automatic control except RTTY or data emissions. Read literally, that would permit a data mesh to carry it. It also means a MeshForge gateway relaying unlicensed 915 MHz users onto 2 m is doing third-party traffic under automatic control, with international-agreement implications (**BELIEVED**, unverified; read §97.115 verbatim before any TX, as the CLAW-1 plan already requires at `.claude/plans/claw1_grilling_2026_11_11.md:15`). Firmware #9958 sidesteps the question by refusing to relay unlicensed traffic. MeshForge needs the same gate.

## Firmware 2.8 is a breaking alpha that MeshForge cannot adopt yet

2.8's headline changes break clients **behaviourally** rather than at the wire. **Node numbers derive from the public key instead of the MAC**, so every node upgrading from 2.7 appears as a new identity. Position and telemetry are **off by default**, and precise position is refused on public channels (#10929). **Packets with missing or invalid `hop_start` are blocked** (#9476). US first-time setup defaults to **LongTurbo**, which cannot hear LongFast (#11637). Long names shrink to 24–25 bytes. The NodeDB storage-format break recommends an erase and reflash ([v2.8.0.47db0e3](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/releases/tag/v2.8.0.47db0e3)). Signing churned between releases. 2.8.1 signs the whole Data envelope and is incompatible with 2.8.0 signatures ([v2.8.1.8e6a88d](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/releases/tag/v2.8.1.8e6a88d)). In 2.8.2, Compatible mode stops signing, AES-CCM takes precedence, MQTT gateways uplink PKI DMs they cannot decrypt (#12110), and meshtasticd gains BlueZ BLE, DMShell, Avahi mDNS and up to 16 admin keys in config.yaml ([v2.8.2.d5f1619](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/releases/tag/v2.8.2.d5f1619)). On the protobuf side, the new `FromRadio` variants are `lockdown_status` (18), `region_presets` (19), `display_frame` (21) and `display_palette` (22). The new fields are `MeshPacket.xeddsa_signed` (22) and `ack_proof_status` (23), plus `Data.xeddsa_signature` (10) ([mesh.proto](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meshtastic/protobufs/master/meshtastic/mesh.proto)). **No 2.8 Python library exists.** PyPI's latest is 2.7.11, with protobufs at about v2.7.26 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/meshtastic/json)). With that library, `RegionCode.Name(28)` on an `ITU2_2M` radio will raise ValueError (**BELIEVED**).

Develop has no 2.9 or 3.0 planning visible. The only release label is "2.8.next". November activity is stabilisation, plus LR11x0/LR2021 CAD-to-LBT work (#12335) and a draft crypto backend rewrite (#12313). No open 2 m PR surfaced on the first page of results ([firmware PRs](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pulls?q=is%3Apr+144)). Each 2.8 alpha has needed a revoke or re-release, so a beta promotion is not imminent (**BELIEVED**, inference). That aligns with MeshForge's standing fleet NEVER: meshtasticd is `apt-mark hold` at 2.7.26 on all nine boxes, and `objdump -T` for `pthread_detach` is required before any move (`.claude/foundations/persistent_issues.md:174-181`). **Any 2 m feature that needs a 2.8 radio on the fleet inherits that gate.**

## Linux meshtasticd in 2.8: more peripherals, and a shell you must turn off

Between 2.7.26 and 2.8.2 the Linux daemon changed more than the radio core did. A read-only clone of the firmware repository was compared at `v2.7.26.54e0d8d`, `v2.8.2.d5f1619` and develop `c7f895df1` (2026-11-14), with nothing built or run. It shows meshtasticd becoming a host-integrated service, with BlueZ BLE, Avahi mDNS, gpsd, libnotify, HUB75 panels, gamepads and native I2C sensors. One addition, DMShell, is a remote login shell that the 2.8.2 tag builds in and switches on. The 2.8.2 tag sits on a release branch that is not an ancestor of develop, so "in 2.8.2" below means "present in the file contents at the tag". Packaging already names the next version: `debian/changelog` on develop carries a `2.8.3.0` entry dated 2026-11-02, with no release date ([debian/changelog](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/debian/changelog)) (**VERIFIED**).

### DMShell: a mesh-reachable login shell, on by default in the 2.8.2 tag

DMShell landed on develop on 2026-10-02 (#11976). It is a shell carried over direct messages ([C/977b1d775](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/commit/977b1d775)). At the 2.8.2 tag it is **unconditional on Linux**. `DMShell.h` defines `MESHTASTIC_HAS_DMSHELL 1` for `ARCH_PORTDUINO && __linux__`, and `src/modules/Modules.cpp:231-233` creates `DMShellModule` under `ARCH_PORTDUINO && MESHTASTIC_HAS_DMSHELL` with no config gate ([Modules.cpp v2.8.2](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/v2.8.2.d5f1619/src/modules/Modules.cpp#L231-L233)) (**VERIFIED** in source).

The authorisation check is the problem. `isAuthorizedPacket()` at `src/modules/DMShell.cpp:476-491` returns `!config.security.is_managed` for `mp.from == 0`, which is the local API. **Any local API client on an unmanaged node is therefore authorised.** A sender on a channel named "admin" is accepted only when `config.security.admin_channel_enabled` is set. Only after those two checks does the code fall through to PKI admin keys ([DMShell.cpp v2.8.2](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/v2.8.2.d5f1619/src/modules/DMShell.cpp#L476-L491)) (**VERIFIED** in source).

Upstream's own fix describes the exposure plainly. Develop commit `e8347ca1b`, "DMShell: make it opt-in and accept admin keys only (#12337)", dated 2026-11-10, says the shell "was built into every Linux build and on by default". The fix adds `Security.DMShell`, default false, and creates the module only when it is true. It refuses local API packets, stops the legacy admin channel from authorising a shell, and accepts only "a PKI DM whose sender key is in AdminKeys" ([C/e8347ca1b](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/commit/e8347ca1b)) (**VERIFIED**).

In practice, anything that can reach TCP 4403 on an unmanaged 2.8.2 node may be able to open a host shell as the `meshtasticd` user, and that includes MeshForge's own API connections (**BELIEVED** from the authorisation code; not exercised). Whether #12337 was cherry-picked to the `2.8.2` release branch after the tag is **UNKNOWN**, because only the tag was inspected. The fleet is held at 2.7.26, tagged 2026-06-23, before DMShell landed, so it is not exposed today. The adoption rule follows directly. Run no 2.8.2-tag meshtasticd on any box. For a bench test, firewall 4403 and set `is_managed`. Adopt only a build that contains #12337, and write `Security.DMShell: false` explicitly into config.yaml so the setting survives any later default change.

## Sensors and telemetry in 2.8+: upstream widens the vocabulary while MeshForge drops most of it

Sensors are where the gap between Meshtastic and MeshForge is widest. Upstream 2.8 adds new telemetry content, new drivers and a Linux path from shell commands to telemetry. It also turns broadcast telemetry off on upgrade and removes MQTT JSON. MeshForge was audited statically at HEAD `7abbb8f`, with no tests run and no live box observed. It partly decodes four of nine telemetry variants, and its MQTT JSON leg probably records nothing. The upstream facts come from read-only clones of `meshtastic/protobufs` (master `a09c0ef`) and firmware develop (`c7f895df1`), both dated 2026-11-14.

### The protobufs add one variant, eighteen environment fields and four sensor types

The `Telemetry` oneof on master is `device_metrics=2, environment_metrics=3, air_quality_metrics=4, power_metrics=5, local_stats=6, health_metrics=7, host_metrics=8, traffic_management_stats=9, soil_water_metrics=11`. Tag 10 is unused ([telemetry.proto master](https://github.com/meshtastic/protobufs/blob/master/meshtastic/telemetry.proto)) (**VERIFIED**). ### Drivers arrive faster than producers

v2.8.0 added drivers for the ADS1X15 ADC, the AS3935 lightning sensor, the DS248X one-wire I²C bridge, HM330X PM, SEN6X, SPA06 and a consolidated SHTXX. It also added an open, in-tree BME680 IAQ estimator that replaces the proprietary Bosch BSEC blob ([#11381](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11381), [#10931](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10931), [#9846](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/9846), [#11390](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11390)). v2.8.2 added `RAKSensorHub` for the RAK2560 WisMesh Hub ([#11817](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11817)), and develop adds the Linux `CommandSensor` described above (**VERIFIED**, `git ls-tree` per tag). **No firmware path emits `SoilWaterMetrics` yet.** The variant appears only in a RAKSensorHub comment ("retarget to soil_water_metrics later"), and the first producer is the open PR [#12279](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12279) (**VERIFIED** by grep; that #12279 is the first producer is **BELIEVED**).

The open-PR queue shows where sensor work is heading. The GitHub search returned 53 matches for "sensor OR telemetry", of which the first 40 were read, by title and date only. They include a shared telemetry scheduler with per-module read intervals ([#12327](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12327)), Modbus-RTU serial mode ([#12064](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12064)), the TI INA228 ([#12123](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12123)), a UART radar level sensor ([#12364](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12364)) and IAQ alert logic ([#11764](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11764)). Three of them, [#12244](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12244) (BME280), [#12370](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12370) (RadSens) and [#12342](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12342) (INA219), stop failed reads from being reported as values. Until they land, a MOC should range-check those sensors (**BELIEVED**). The noise floor has a similar history. "Noise floor (#9347)" added sampling in 2.8, and #11749 stopped broadcasting the −120 dBm placeholder. Because `LocalStats.noise_floor` has no presence bit, it is now 0 when no samples exist ([#11749](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11749)) (**VERIFIED**). A MOC should therefore read **0, and −120 from a 2.8.0 node, as "not measured"**, never as a noise floor (**BELIEVED**).

### 2.8 turns broadcast telemetry off on upgrade, and JSON off on MQTT

Every 2.8 tag runs `optInDisableTelemetryBroadcast()` once, gated on `moduleConfig.version < POSITION_TELEMETRY_OPTIN_VER`. It sets `device_telemetry_enabled`, `environment_measurement_enabled`, `air_quality_enabled`, `power_measurement_enabled` and `health_measurement_enabled` to false, and it clears `should_report_location` in the MQTT map report. The code comment says it "does NOT re-clobber a user who later re-enables sharing" ([NodeDB.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/mesh/NodeDB.cpp)) (**VERIFIED**). The PR that introduced this opt-in flip was not identified, so the #10929 attribution in the firmware section above is unconfirmed for telemetry. Telemetry still reaches the locally connected client: with `device_telemetry_enabled` off, `DeviceTelemetry.cpp` sends to the phone path only, the same gate as in 2.7.26 ([DeviceTelemetry.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/modules/Telemetry/DeviceTelemetry.cpp)). That BLE/serial phone path is VERIFIED. That the TCP 4403 path behaves identically is **BELIEVED**.

After an upgrade, a fleet of 2.8 nodes therefore goes quiet on mesh and MQTT telemetry by configuration, not by failure. A MOC must not report that as "dark" (**BELIEVED**). The default broadcast interval is 1 h (12 h for routers), with a 30-minute floor ([Default.h](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/mesh/Default.h)). 2.8 multiplies the interval by the region profile's `telemetryThrottle`, which is **×2 on the 2 m `PROFILE_HAM_20KHZ`**, ×1 on 1.25 m and 70 cm, and ×10 on EU "Lite" ([Default.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/mesh/Default.cpp)). Device, environment, health and power telemetry pass `TrafficType::TELEMETRY`. AirQualityTelemetry calls the overload without it, so **air-quality intervals are not region-throttled** ([AirQualityTelemetry.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/modules/Telemetry/AirQualityTelemetry.cpp)) (**VERIFIED**). On 2 m the effective default becomes 2 h with a 60-minute floor, so telemetry age thresholds should scale by region (**BELIEVED**). Traffic Management rate-limits every portnum except ROUTING and ADMIN, so TELEMETRY_APP from a chatty node can be dropped and not relayed. Each drop is counted in `TrafficManagementStats.rate_limit_drops` ([TrafficManagementModule.cpp](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/modules/TrafficManagementModule.cpp)) (**VERIFIED**). That counter is exactly the signal that tells congestion apart from silence.

**MQTT JSON is gone from every 2.8 build.** #10152 deleted the JSON code from `MQTT.cpp`: the count of `json` occurrences in that file is 80 at v2.7.26 and 0 at v2.8.0, v2.8.2 and develop. `MeshPacketSerializer` survives only under `ARCH_PORTDUINO`, for LOG_TRACE and the `JSONFilename` file output ([#10152](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/10152)) (**VERIFIED**). The surviving serializer also ignores lightning, ADC, one-wire, `pm_status_flags` and soil/water fields ([MeshPacketSerializer.cpp develop](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/develop/src/serialization/MeshPacketSerializer.cpp)). A consumer of `msh/.../json/...` topics gets nothing from a 2.8 node and must decode protobuf ServiceEnvelopes (**BELIEVED**).

Signing changed again late in the cycle. #12191 (2026-10-22) made Compatible, the default, unsigned. Balanced and Strict sign broadcasts, and licensed nodes also sign unicasts under those two modes. Each signature costs 66 bytes, AES-CCM packets are never signed, and only Strict drops unsigned packets ([#12191](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/12191)) (**VERIFIED** from the commit body). Whether #12191 is in the 2.8.2 tag is **BELIEVED** from its date; ancestry was not checked.

**Ham telemetry is a blank upstream too.** `git log --grep='aprs\|swr'` and `grep -rli aprs src` on develop return nothing. There is no APRS-style weather or telemetry, no SWR and no station-power feature (**VERIFIED** absence). The closest building blocks are AS3935 lightning and the ADS1X15's eight ADC channels, one of which could carry forward or reflected voltage, but nothing computes SWR. Licensed mode strips encryption, so ham telemetry is cleartext on air by design. Under default Compatible signing it is also unsigned, so its integrity depends on the operator choosing Balanced or Strict (**BELIEVED**).

### MeshForge's MQTT JSON reader expects a shape that firmware never sends

Four ingest legs decode TELEMETRY_APP, and none keeps the whole packet. The coordinator confirmed the most serious defect by reading both sides. Firmware v2.7.26 `src/serialization/MeshPacketSerializer.cpp:66-89` writes telemetry JSON as **flat** keys: `msgPayload["battery_level"]`, `["temperature"]`, `["barometric_pressure"]` and so on, with air quality as `pm10`/`pm25`/`pm100` ([MeshPacketSerializer.cpp v2.7.26](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/blob/v2.7.26.54e0d8d/src/serialization/MeshPacketSerializer.cpp#L66-L89)). MeshForge's `src/monitoring/_mqtt_message_decoder.py:505` reads the nested `payload.get("device_metrics", {})` with no flat fallback, and `:545` expects `pm25_standard` (**VERIFIED** by code read). The test fixtures enshrine the nested shape (`tests/test_mqtt_robustness.py:276-282, 399-447`), so the suite cannot catch the mismatch. The gateway bridge's MQTT telemetry handler reads the same nested keys and only logs them (`src/gateway/mqtt_bridge_handler.py:1003-1015`). The same file's position handler reads flat `payload.latitude_i` (`:1022-1024`), which shows that the flat shape is known.

The likely live consequence is that **no MQTT JSON telemetry is recorded at all**. Kilo, the Prometheus and InfluxDB exporters, the TUI MQTT node view and the low-battery alert all read `MQTTNode` fields that this leg never fills (**BELIEVED**). The check is `mosquitto_sub -t 'msh/+/2/json/#' -C 5` on a box with JSON uplink, plus a count of environment rows in `kilo_telemetry.db` (`SELECT metric,count(*) FROM readings WHERE transport='mqtt' GROUP BY metric`). Zero env rows against flat traffic on the wire would confirm it. Because 2.8 removes MQTT JSON entirely, **the fix is to decode TELEMETRY_APP from the protobuf ServiceEnvelope on the MQTT leg, not to patch JSON keys**. The encrypted `/e/` leg already decrypts packets, but it keeps only those with `result.get("text")` and swallows the rest with `except Exception: pass` (`_mqtt_message_decoder.py:129-148`). That is the natural hook for the fix.

The TCP leg, `MessageListener._handle_telemetry` (`src/utils/message_listener.py:539-628`), is the only path that fills the tracker's `Telemetry`, and it has five bugs of its own. The guard `if temp or humidity or pressure:` drops 0.0 °C as "no reading" (`:551`). Pressure is written to `node.telemetry.barometric_pressure` (`:566`), but the dataclass field is `pressure` (`src/gateway/node_models.py:352`), so `to_dict()` never serialises it. Air quality is dropped unless a telemetry object already exists, and it is also dropped at zero (`:577, 587`). Device metrics are skipped when battery is absent (`:604`). Telemetry from nodes the tracker does not yet know is discarded (`:560, 612`). On top of that, **no leg sets `telemetry.timestamp`**: a grep for `telemetry.timestamp =` in `src` returns nothing. `UnifiedNode.from_meshtastic` stamps NodeDB metrics with `datetime.now()` rather than the reading's own time (`node_models.py:875-882`).

Power, local_stats, host_metrics and traffic_management_stats are dropped on every leg, and they have all been in protobufs since v2.7.26. The traffic inspector's dissector shows `deviceMetrics` only (`src/monitoring/packet_dissectors.py:258-269`). The `Telemetry` model has no PowerMetrics, LocalStats, HostMetrics or extended-environment fields (`node_models.py:210-280, 329-435`). A grep for `xeddsa|traffic_management|soil|lightning|wind_speed|lux` finds no ingestion code.

### Storage, display and management stop short of a sensor MOC

**No general per-node sensor history exists.** `node_history.node_observations` keeps battery and voltage for 48 hours, and `record_observations()` requires lat/lon (`src/utils/node_history.py:115, 203-231, 596`). Position-less sensor nodes, which become the 2.8 default, never get a row. Kilo's `readings` table is a well-designed `(ts, transport, node_key, metric, value)` series with 30-day retention and a DBSpec (`src/utils/db_inventory.py:78-92`, `src/kilo/store.py:61-95`). It covers only 12 metrics, for registered lab nodes, sourced from the broken MQTT leg, and it admits that "MQTTNode carries no per-metric timestamps" (`src/kilo/ingest.py:22-46, 94-110`). `metrics.db` is a generic rollup store, and no caller was found that writes per-node sensor values into it.

Sensors appear on five surfaces: the TUI MQTT node detail, the MQTT GeoJSON and NOC web map, the folium popup (battery only), Prometheus and InfluxDB. **None shows the age of a reading.** The TUI gates are truthy (`has_env = node.temperature or …`, `if node.voltage:`), so 0 °C, and the 0.0 V that USB-powered nodes really report, are hidden (`src/launcher_tui/handlers/mqtt.py:622-703`). The web map gets null handling right (`web/node_map.html:2957-2966`). The exporters use `is not None` checks, but they filter by node `last_seen`, which any packet type refreshes (`src/utils/prometheus_exporter.py:644-700`, `src/utils/influxdb_exporter.py:419-445`). A sensor that died hours ago therefore keeps exporting a fresh-looking gauge. The changelog's "Full telemetry pipeline — sensor data through Prometheus, InfluxDB, Grafana" (`src/__version__.py:212`) has not been verified against real firmware output.

**Management is a primitive with no UI.** `ProtobufAdmin` can read and set module config 5 (telemetry) in a begin/commit transaction (`src/gateway/_protobuf_admin.py:214-258, 305-330`), but no TUI, utils or commands caller was found. The `configure_telemetry()` form in `src/config/modules.py:277-314` collects intervals into a dict and never writes them to a device. The meshtasticd YAML editor covers `HostMetrics` and `I2CDevice` only (`src/core/meshtasticd_config.py:171-188`, `src/config/yaml_editor.py:757-796`). `TelemetryPoller`, which is off by default, shells out to `meshtastic --request-telemetry` (`src/utils/telemetry_poller.py:333-428`). Because the TCP leg never stamps a timestamp, it treats nodes as "No telemetry ever — definitely should poll" (`:204-206`) and probably re-polls the same nodes every cycle. Its CLI subprocess also opens a TCP client outside `MESHTASTIC_CONNECTION_LOCK`, a possible Issue #29 regression class (both **BELIEVED**).

### MeshCore, RNS and what 2.8 breaks

MeshCore sensor data is not ingested. ## MeshForge would mis-tune, hide licensing and could encrypt onto 2 m

The readiness audit found five classes of gap. I re-read the load-bearing lines this session at HEAD `7abbb8f`.

**The slot math is wrong for ham profiles, beyond simply lacking the region.** `src/utils/meshtastic_modem.py:25` pins `FIRMWARE_TAG = "v2.7.26.54e0d8d"`, and `REGIONS` (`:61-77`) holds 25 sub-GHz regions with no 2 m entry. More important, `slot_centre_mhz()` returns `start + bw_mhz / 2 + idx * bw_mhz` (`:115-126`), and `num_channels()` divides by `spacing + BW` (`:109-112`) (**VERIFIED**, read this session). The 2.8 formula adds `2*padding` to the slot width, adds `padding` to the centre, and defaults to a per-region `override_slot` rather than the djb2 channel-name hash. If someone simply added `ITU2_2M` to `REGIONS`, MeshForge would compute 256 slots instead of 200. Slot 51 would land at 144 + 0.0078 + 50 × 0.0156 = **144.788 MHz instead of 145.010 MHz**, a 222 kHz error, and the default slot would come from a hash instead of 51 (my arithmetic on verified code). The fix is structural: a profile layer with padding and an override slot, not just a new row.

**Six drifted region tables surround the one accurate table.** These are `src/standalone.py:274-313` (still carrying `UK_868`/`PH`, with an off-by-one slot count), `src/config/lora.py:22-70` (which forces US at `:245-246`), `src/utils/lora_presets.py:153-190`, `src/launcher_tui/handlers/radio_menu.py:352-384`, `src/launcher_tui/handlers/first_run.py:119-140`, and the stale `ComplianceChecker.MESHTASTIC_PRESETS` at `src/amateur/compliance.py:523-530`. Only `src/launcher_tui/handlers/rf_tools.py:81-92` derives from `meshtastic_modem.REGIONS`. Per honest-failure rule 5, two consumers of one artifact share one constant, and here seven do not.

**RF defaults silently assume 915 MHz.** The physics in `src/utils/rf.py` is frequency-generic: FSPL at `:461-473` and knife-edge at `:528-564`. The defaults are not: `:398`, `:529`, `:601`, `:696` and `:988` default to 906.875 or 915 MHz, as do `src/utils/terrain.py:816,938` and `src/utils/preset_impact.py:68-69`. `calculate_cable_loss(cable_type, length_m, connectors)` at `src/utils/rf.py:351` takes **no frequency argument** (**VERIFIED**), so a 146 MHz link budget gets 915 MHz coax loss, overstating loss by roughly √(915/146) ≈ 2.5× (scaling **BELIEVED**). `ENVIRONMENT_PARAMS` and `BUILDING_PENETRATION_DB` (`:52-85`) are 868/915 field data, and `DEFAULT_NOISE_FLOOR_DBM = -120.0` (`:226`) is optimistic against the ~13 dB residential VHF noise figure. `tests/test_rf.py` has no case between 144 and 148 MHz. The first Fresnel radius is about 2.5× larger at 146 MHz, so terrain LOS verdicts that keep the 915 default would be silently wrong.

**Licensing is parsed and then thrown away.** `is_licensed` appears only in `src/monitoring/node_monitor.py:88,423`. `set_owner` in `src/gateway/_protobuf_admin.py:452-454` never writes it, and nothing in `src` sets `override_frequency` (it is read once, at `src/commands/rnode.py:635,653-654`). The amateur band-plan view filters to `lora_ranges = ((420.0, 450.0), (902.0, 928.0))` at `src/launcher_tui/handlers/amateur_radio.py:152` (**VERIFIED**), so **2 m is never shown**. The §97.113 rule entry at `src/amateur/compliance.py:169-216` omits the encryption ban. The TUI compliance check (`amateur_radio.py:172-230`) scrapes CLI text and swallows exceptions. `StationIDTimer` (`src/amateur/callsign.py:715-830`) is unwired and uses wall-clock `datetime.now()`, which violates honest-failure rule 6.

**The bridge has no amateur regime.** `MeshBridgeConfig` legs carry `preset`, `downlink_psk` and `injection_mode` but no regime or callsign (`src/gateway/config.py:268-381`). Downlink injection AES-CTR-encrypts with a required 32-byte PSK (`src/gateway/mqtt_downlink_inject.py:207-321`). `src/utils/rnode_profile.py:68` accepts anything from 137 MHz to 3 GHz as a hardware check only (**VERIFIED**), so an always-encrypted RNS interface on 145 MHz validates without warning. Encrypted RNS on 145 MHz is the §97.113 problem in its purest form. `docs/birc_2026_05_17_outline.md:196` ("Run it on Part 97 frequencies, follow ID rules") contradicts this and should be corrected. One unrelated bug also turned up: `src/monitoring/packet_dissectors.py:234` labels portnum 4 as POSITION and `:246` labels portnum 5 as NODEINFO, while the project's own table at `src/monitoring/traffic_models.py:73-75` maps 3 = POSITION, 4 = NODEINFO, 5 = ROUTING (**VERIFIED**, read this session).

## Conclusion

Meshtastic solved 2 m as a **policy problem**: licensed-only regions, PSK stripping, signed plaintext, an unlicensed-relay block and a 20 kHz raster that respects the US cap. Physics and coordination are left to the operator. The silicon story pins the whole ecosystem to the aging SX127x, there is no hardware gate against mis-tuning, there is no field data, and the R1 default sits outside RSGB's 12 kHz digital segment. MeshForge's value as a "tell the truth when it cannot see" MOC is clearest in those same blind spots. It can compute the right slot with padding, flag a 915 MHz board told to tune 145 MHz, refuse an encrypted leg on an amateur frequency, and label every VHF link estimate UNMEASURED until a both-ends test exists.

The counterintuitive takeaway is that 2 m matters to MeshForge **before** any 2 m radio joins the fleet. The RNode Part 97 warning, the §97.113 rule text, the slot-formula fix and the portnum bug are all live defects today on 2.7.26, and none adds an edge. The same holds for sensors: the MQTT telemetry decoder, the TCP-leg zero and pressure bugs, and the missing reading age are 2.7.26 defects, and 2.8's JSON removal and telemetry opt-out only make them louder. The 2.8 bump, which is already owed for the NodeNum re-key, is the gate that 2 m adoption inherits. The amateur bridge leg belongs after BIC-6, where a band-diverse 145 MHz path that survives 915 MHz interference bursts becomes the best argument for measured best-available-route.
