Cascadia Mesh, studied from Hawaiʻi

The Pacific Northwest's community MeshCore network: what it runs, why its website pulls people in, what MeshForge should borrow, and the ways we can join from 4,000 km of open water away.

What Cascadia Mesh is

A volunteer community that builds and maintains a MeshCore LoRa network across British Columbia, Washington and Oregon, and lately Eastern Washington and North Idaho as well. They work with local clubs to put solar repeaters on high points. Meshtastic is not their stack. Third-party write-ups quote PNW operators who moved to MeshCore because Meshtastic's 7-hop flood limit fell apart at their scale. BELIEVED

The operating rules that keep a 3,700-node mesh usable

  • Region tags on every repeater. A Seattle repeater carries west, pnw, wa, w-wa, sea. Matching is per tag, not hierarchical: carrying wa does not forward sea traffic, so every ancestor tag has to be set explicitly. On firmware 1.15+, adding a region turns flooding on for it. On 1.14 and older you also need a per-region allow-flood command.
  • Default region only stamps the repeater's own packets (mainly its adverts). It does not change what the repeater forwards.
  • Users scope their channels. Companion apps have "Set Region Scope" on a channel, so local chat stays on the metro tag and statewide chat uses the state tag.
  • Advert discipline. Flood adverts at least every 70 hours; zero-hop adverts can stay short (8 h or more) for neighbour discovery; set loop.detect minimal. VERIFIED live page. Corrected: the first pass said 48 h and a zero-hop interval of 0.
  • Regions are a proposal, rolled out in two phases. Phase 1 (tags, default region, scoped channels) is safe at any pace because unscoped traffic keeps flooding. Phase 2 is a breaking change: the repeater stops forwarding unscoped traffic, and any neighbour who has not scoped their channels can no longer pass traffic through it. Firmware 1.16 adds a one-message bulk region load (MeshCore PR #2540, merged 2026-05-23). VERIFIED
  • Corridor projects. The I-5 Project is building a continuous repeater spine from Olympia to Vancouver, WA. The first MeshAmerica repeater is up near Mayfield. Hosts sign up via Discord or email.

MediaMesh and the web flasher

Added 2026-10-31 at WH6GXZ's request. This is the most technically interesting thing Cascadia ships. Their own page (cascadiamesh.org/mediamesh-flasher) was blocked here too; the flasher itself is served from jade-on-mesh.github.io/wadamesh-tak. BELIEVED

What MediaMesh is

MediaMesh puts photos and voice clips on a second, faster LoRa profile ("radio2") on the same SX1262, while text stays on the main 910.525 MHz mesh. Bulk media never eats the shared channel's airtime. That's the same airtime discipline as their 70 h advert rule, applied to media. The live flasher page describes radio2 as "250 kHz fast or 125 kHz range, and community mesh channels are refused": the firmware will not let radio2 sit on a shared mesh channel. VERIFIED. The 911.150 MHz default from one snapshot does not appear on the live page. UNKNOWN

  • Stock MeshCore does not have radio2. Users on stock 1.18.0 companions (T1000-E, Heltec v3) get "This radio's firmware has no MediaMesh (radio2)."
  • Upstreaming is in progress: meshcore-dev/MeshCore#3638 (mikecarper) adds dual radio profiles and scheduled switching. It is open, not merged: +5,417 / −277 lines, opened 2026-10-20, last updated 10-26. Stock MeshCore is at 1.18.0 (2026-10-15). VERIFIED via GitHub. Today radio2 lives only in their forks.
  • Also on the page: a "900 MHz link scan" that finds drones without Remote ID by their ELRS, Crossfire or SiK control links. Receive-only, but outside our domain; noted, not adopted.
  • Analyzer support: Kpa-clawbot/CoreScope#2360 upstreams MediaMesh decoding.

What it means for us

  • A second MeshCore channel exists in the band. Our SDR channel table (src/utils/sdr_analysis.py) knows only 910.525. If radio2 traffic shows up on a waterfall near 911.15 it is MediaMesh, not interference. Add it as a labelled channel once the value is confirmed.
  • Region-tuned forks are a fork-pin problem we already solved. Running Keymind Cascade means pinning a fork by tag and SHA and reading the pinned source, which is the same discipline as our RNS/LXMF forks. Don't adopt it until #3638's fate is known.
  • Heltec v3 caution. radio2 on a v3 adds TX on a board we've measured self-interfering. Bench one before trusting it. UNKNOWN for MeshCore.
  • Hawaiʻi lesson: a role-based web flasher with rollback is the single most adoptable piece for a new island community. We'd point at theirs (or the stock MeshCore flasher) before building our own.

Critical assessment for our domain

Added in the second pass. The first pass asked "what does Cascadia do that we don't?" This section asks the harder question: which of it fits how this domain actually runs? Each point is checked against the repo, the fleet, or a recorded operator decision.

1 · Most of this belongs in MeshAnchor, not MeshForge

VERIFIED

MeshAnchor is the MeshCore-primary sister, split from MeshForge on 2026-04-01. In MeshForge, MeshCore is an optional gateway handler. Region-tag awareness, the advert audit, observer ingest and the prefix bot are MeshCore-native features. Their first home is MeshAnchor, and MeshForge's optional handler follows through the twin map. A grep of MeshAnchor finds no region-tag or MeshCore observer code either, so the gap is real in both repos. The adopt list below now says so.

2 · We run no MeshCore repeaters

VERIFIED

docs/fleet_roles.yaml has no repeater role. The fleet's MeshCore presence is the MeshAnchor box's radio, a companion by design (operator). A companion still sees every packet raw (0x88 push), so measurement needs no repeater. So "audit the repeaters we manage" audits nothing today. The real value of a region and advert Config Doctor is as a standalone tool for the Hawaiʻi MeshCore operators who do run repeaters. That fits the standalone bar: a ham without Claude diagnoses their own repeater from the TUI.

3 · The Phase 2 trap is our defect class, and the strongest reason to build

VERIFIED

A Phase 2 repeater looks configured, stays up, and silently drops every unscoped neighbour. A leaf tag without its ancestors does the same thing more quietly. That is honest-failure-modes #1–2: a degraded state that reads healthy. A checker that says "this repeater is Phase 2 while N neighbours are unscoped" is exactly the kind of truth-telling MeshForge exists for.

4 · A Hawaiʻi region scheme is not ours to coin

VERIFIED operator record

There is already a Hawaiʻi MeshCore community, new to this and using it for emergency comms. The operator knows some of them, and the band's future is their decision. The table below stays a draft to bring to them, not something to deploy. Ask whether hawaiimesh.net or the Big Island group already has a convention before proposing one.

5 · Publishing to LetsMesh sends Hawaiʻi traffic to a mainland public broker

BELIEVED

An observer that publishes to a shared public broker exposes the local community's traffic metadata (who, when, which repeaters) to anyone. We have a precedent: the 2026-05 Channel 0 leak on MeshAnchor. Our rule is refuse-by-default for egress. Default the observer to the local broker only; publishing outward is the community's consent to give, per site.

6 · The US preset itself is contested

VERIFIED issue state

MeshCore#945, "Suggestion: new USA Regulatory preset", is open with 377 comments. It challenges whether the narrow 62.5 kHz US/Canada preset fits the US rules. We take no position: it is a legal question, and the band's direction is a community decision. But MeshForge should show which preset a radio uses, never imply it is compliant.

7 · The front door belongs to the community; MeshForge feeds it the truth

BELIEVED

Cascadia's site is a community site, not a NOC tool. For Hawaiʻi that role belongs to the community (hawaiimesh.net). MeshForge's part is an honest data feed: node counts with their age, a coverage map that says where it cannot see. That keeps MeshForge on its own job, telling the truth, instead of becoming a second community site.

CoreScope: the sandbox, and how we become part of it

Added 2026-10-31. CoreScope is the open MeshCore analyzer the community already uses: Go, GPL-3.0, about 200 stars, pushed daily. It ingests packets over MQTT from observers, decodes them and serves a public read API. It already does the region work this study proposed we build. VERIFIED source, API spec and a live instance

472repeaters in the public instance's Scope Audit (BE/NL/DE)
36already in Phase 2 (no-unscoped)
0Hawaiʻi packets on any CoreScope: we have no observer

One read-only GET each to analyzer.on8ar.eu/api/scope-audit and /api/scope-stats, 2026-10-31 ~08:45 HST. VERIFIED

What it already has

  • Per-packet route type on /api/packets and /api/decode: routeType, transportCodes and the raw hex. The decoder matches MeshCore's packet_format.md exactly.
  • Scope Audit (/api/scope-audit, CoreScope #1975): for every repeater, the regions it declares against the regions it was heard forwarding. configState includes no-unscoped, which is Phase 2. Also observedUnscopedPackets and notObserved.
  • The same honesty creed as ours. Their notes say "never asked" is not "declared nothing", that an absent field is not evidence, and they name each blind spot. We would be joining people who already think the way we do.

Two small defects we found (documentation, not architecture)

  • The API contract mislabels route types. proto/decoded.proto says 0=DIRECT, 1=FLOOD, 2=reserved, 3=TRANSPORT; the code and MeshCore's spec say 0=TRANSPORT_FLOOD, 1=FLOOD, 2=DIRECT, 3=TRANSPORT_DIRECT. A client built from the contract would misread packets. VERIFIED
  • /api/scope-stats breaks its own definition. The spec says transportTotal = scoped + unscoped; the live response read transportTotal 7626, scoped 7621, unscoped 14328. unscoped likely also counts plain FLOOD packets. VERIFIED one response · cause UNKNOWN

Both are findings to report in WH6GXZ's own words, as issues: small, checkable, and proof we read their code closely. No AI-written code or PR text under his name.

How we become part of it

  1. Become a data source. The MeshAnchor box's companion already receives every raw packet (push 0x88). MeshAnchor publishes them in the meshcoretomqtt topic shape (meshcore/{IATA}/{PUBKEY}/packets) to a local broker. No new hardware, no custom firmware.
  2. Run a Hawaiʻi CoreScope. Self-host one fed by that broker. Hawaiʻi then appears in the same tool Belgium and the Bay Area use, with the Scope Audit working on our mesh from day one.
  3. Share outward with consent. Publishing to a shared or public broker is the Hawaiʻi MeshCore community's decision, site by site. Refuse by default.
  4. MeshForge reads, never rebuilds. The MOC view pulls CoreScope's API (Scope Audit, node health) and shows it beside Meshtastic, RNS and AREDN. That multi-mesh view is the thing nobody else has, and it is what MeshForge offers back.
  5. Contribute. File the two findings. Later, where Hawaiʻi's island geography exposes something new (sparse links, inter-island hops), bring it as data from our instance.

What to adopt

Ranked by value to the fleet over effort. "Where" names the MeshForge file that would change. Nothing here is built yet.

1

Region-tag and Phase 2 check: feed CoreScope, don't rebuild it

now

Read and display the region tags a repeater carries, and flag the classic mistake: a leaf tag without its ancestors, which silently stops forwarding. This is a Config Doctor rule in the same family as our existing drift checks. The trap matches our own creed: a repeater that looks configured but does not forward.

Where: MeshAnchor first (MeshCore-primary), then MeshForge's optional src/gateway/meshcore_handler.py through the twin map. Config Doctor and the TUI MeshCore menu. Flag Phase 2 when neighbours are unscoped. Test first (tdd skill).

2

MeshCore MQTT observer ingest

now

Subscribe to meshcore/{IATA}/+/packets and feed packets into the node map and traffic inspector, the same way the Meshtastic MQTT leg works. This gives every box visibility of MeshCore traffic it can't hear on RF, and makes our observer interoperable with LetsMesh, CoreScope and meshcore-analyzer.

Where: src/monitoring/ mqtt subscriber + dissectors, web/node_map.html. Needs a DBSpec if it persists (MF013).

3

Advert-interval audit

next

Flag a repeater whose flood-advert interval is under 70 h, or that has no loop detection. Cheap, and it's the norm the largest MeshCore community in the US runs on. We run no repeaters, so this serves community operators using the standalone tool.

Where: Config Doctor rule beside #1.

4

A public "Get on the mesh" page

next

A read-only newcomer page served beside the node map: three steps, the preset, channel QR codes, a node counter that says how fresh it is. Our version should show its age honestly ("312 nodes, as of 4 min ago"), something Cascadia's cached counter doesn't do.

Where: new web/ page, reusing map data. Strip LAN IPs (MF015).

5

Corridor planning, Hawaiʻi style

later

Their I-5 spine maps onto our island problems: H-1 and H-3 on Oʻahu, Saddle Road across Hawaiʻi Island, and the inter-island hops that only high sites can bridge. MeshForge already has coverage maps and LOS. A "corridor" view that strings sites together and shows the gaps is the missing piece.

Where: src/utils/coverage_map.py, web/los_visualization.html.

7

Recognise MediaMesh radio2 traffic

later

Label radio2 as a known channel in the SDR tools, and decode MediaMesh packets in the MeshCore observer leg once CoreScope's decoder lands. Watch MeshCore#3638 before considering a fork.

Where: src/utils/sdr_analysis.py, MeshCore dissector (adopt item 2).

6

Repeater prefix lookup bot command

later

HowlBot answers "who is prefix A3?" in-band. Useful when reading paths off a MeshCore trace. Small add once #2 gives us a node table.

Where: src/plugins/ bot plugin.

A Hawaiʻi region plan

A draft to bring to the Hawaiʻi community, not a plan to deploy (critique #4). It follows Cascadia's pattern so their tools work unchanged. Phase 1 only. Phase 2 waits until every island's channels are scoped.
